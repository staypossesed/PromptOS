# Umprompt: first useful task — facilitator review card

Purpose: assess whether an adult new to AI can complete one ordinary task. This is a proposed review protocol, not a completed experiment or evidence of efficacy.

## Before inviting learners

1. A facilitator privately reviews the site and commercial limits. Do not describe Umprompt as a free public service or affiliated with the facilitator's organization.
2. Test access on the planned device/network before any group session. One guest task and shared-network limits can make a classroom trial unsuitable without a separately agreed setup.
3. Use fictional names, dates, employers and commitments. Do not submit medical records, private messages, account credentials or participant lists.
4. Participation is voluntary. No account is needed for the guest task. Explain that saving or continuing requires an account, free accounts include seven weekly ideas, and paid plans exist.
5. Explain that submitted tasks are processed by Umprompt's AI provider. No facilitator should promise correctness, accessibility compliance, confidentiality or exam success.

## Exercise A: a message someone can review

Start: “Help me ask my manager for two extra days to finish a report. I can deliver Friday instead of Wednesday. Keep it short and polite. Do not invent a reason.”

The learner decides whether to prepare instructions for their own AI assistant or get a first draft here. Ask them to explain that choice in their own words before proceeding.

Observe without coaching first. Can they find where to add details, see the result and use Edit or Copy? If help is needed, record which step needed it; assisted completion is different from independent completion.

Review checklist:

- The requested dates were preserved.
- No excuse or personal fact was invented.
- The tone is appropriate for the intended recipient.
- Any placeholders are obvious.
- The person can edit a sentence and confirm the copied text reflects their edit.
- Nothing was sent to a manager automatically.

A copied result with an invented reason is not success. Editing to remove it and checking the final text may be a successful assisted result; record that accurately.

## Exercise B: plan a manageable week

Use as an alternative, not a second guest task if the trial allowance has been consumed.

Start: “Help me plan next week. I work Monday to Friday, 9 to 5, want two 30-minute walks, one grocery trip and some free time. Ask about missing fixed commitments instead of inventing them.”

Check that the plan respects the stated work hours and doesn't fill all free time. The learner decides whether it is realistic. Do not collect the person's actual calendar.

## Exercise C: practise an interview

Alternative task with fictional role and experience. Ask for one question at a time, wait for the answer, then offer feedback. If producing instructions for another AI assistant, completion in Umprompt is preparation only: the learner must still use the instructions and judge the resulting practice before calling the overall task successful.

## Record outcomes without private content

Use a random participant code local to the facilitator, not a name or email. Do not send participant-level data to Umprompt without a separate appropriate agreement. Aggregate observations are enough for an initial review.

For each voluntary attempt record:

- Started / completed independently / completed with help / could not complete.
- Chosen route: instructions for another assistant or draft here.
- First point of confusion, in general language, without private task content.
- Result reviewed: yes/no.
- Learner says the result was useful: yes/no/not sure.
- Used or tested it in the intended workflow: yes/no/not observed.
- Any access limit, language issue or unsupported expectation.

No sample size or conversion target is asserted here. A two- or three-person pilot gives qualitative observations, not a population-wide success rate. Declining to sign up or buy is a legitimate outcome.

## What to do with feedback

If a facilitator is interested: agree the review scope and access first, then invite willing learners through the facilitator. Do not obtain their mailing list. If a confusing step repeats, fix and retest before asking for a larger audience. Never ask the facilitator to claim endorsement, submit favorable reviews or promise a number of paying users.

No testimonial or screenshot with personal data may be published without the person's specific permission. No paid terms, discounts, contracts, scheduling or group-account promises are authorized by this review card.
